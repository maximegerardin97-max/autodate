iphonectl

Use your iPhone from your Mac: a live view you can click, type and scroll on, an assistant you can ask to do things on the phone, and the Hinge swiping helper that learns your taste.

Download iphonectl for Mac

Version 0.1.0 · 129 MB zip · Apple Silicon, macOS 14+

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 14 or newer.
  • Your iPhone and a cable to plug it into the Mac.
  • An Apple ID (a free one is fine) and about 15 GB of free space (for Xcode, Apple's free developer app).

1. Install

  1. Unzip the download, then drag iphonectl into your Applications folder.
  2. Open it from Applications. macOS says it can't check it for malicious software (it isn't from the App Store): click Done.
  3. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down to “iphonectl was blocked…”, click Open Anyway, enter your Mac password, then Open. You only do this once.

2. Set up (the app guides you)

A setup window checks each step by itself and moves on when it's done:

  1. Install Xcode from the App Store (free, big download), then let the app finish its setup (it asks for your Mac password once).
  2. Sign in to Xcode with your Apple ID: Xcode › Settings… › Accounts › +.
  3. Plug in your iPhone, unlock it, and tap Trust.
  4. Turn on Developer Mode on the iPhone: Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode. It restarts; then tap Turn On.
  5. The app installs a small helper app on your iPhone (a few minutes the first time). When the iPhone asks, trust it: Settings › General › VPN & Device Management › your Apple ID › Trust.

That's it: from then on, open iphonectl with your iPhone plugged in and it connects by itself.

Every 7 days, Apple's free signing of the helper app runs out. iphonectl renews it on its own the next time your iPhone is plugged in; if your iPhone asks you to trust it again, do.

Good to know

  • Privacy: your phone's screen stays on your Mac, except when the assistant or the swiping helper needs to decide something: then screenshots of the screens it's working on are sent to Anthropic's Claude to decide. Your swiping preferences are stored only on your Mac.
  • Keep the iPhone unlocked while you use it from the Mac; it can't unlock it for you.
  • If something stops working, the app shows what to fix at the top of the window.
Hinge and other dating apps forbid automated use in their terms, and can ban accounts that use it. The swiping helper's Auto mode is automation: use it at your own risk.
